## Who gets to issue credentials?

In any [framework for Digital Trust Infrastructure](https://cardanofoundation.org/resource-library/introducing-digital-trust-infrastructure-bri) that specifies a need for digital credentials, we encounter a common question: "Who has authority to issue or vouch for what?" (BRI 18). 

The default answer is gatekeeping. A "trust registry" can list "authorized issuers," governance bodies can decide who joins the list, and credentials derive value from issuer authority. This works for high-stakes credentials, like professional licenses, government IDs, and university degrees.

However. The most valuable credentials for the ways that globally-networked communities will work together do not exist yet. They'll come from community organizations teaching practical skills, open-source projects recognizing contributions, and teams experimenting with novel collaboration. Approval from a governance body may be necessary in some cases, but too often, it only introduces new gatekeepers.

Andamio offers a different path. **Anyone can issue credentials. Value is emergent, determined by the network, not by global authorities.**

## Local Authority, Global Network

If decentralization promises anything, it's that local communities matter. Action happens in small spaces: teams, cohorts, organizations, collectives. While AI draws people into agentic bubbles and social media algorithms optimize for engagement, Andamio empowers small groups to share knowledge, allocate resources, and solve locally-relevant problems.

A blockchain transaction is an attestation that something happened and is true. Changing on-chain data requires a human or agent to sign, and signing requires sufficient reason. Andamio makes it easier for people to add attestations to what they have learned and accomplished in local spaces where validation is straightforward, then share these attestations across a global network where they unlock new opportunities.

## Building Great Teams

We wrap the concept of local-ness in the idea of "teams." A team is any group of people who come together to collaborate—always local in some way: to a place, a company, an online collective.

The world needs greater levels of trust. The problems we face require collaboration. Andamio provides infrastructure that integrates with existing platforms while giving collectives of people the tools to build new ones. Andamio allows users to carry a professional identity across platforms, projects, and communities—building reputation based on real commitments and interactions.

## What Andamio Provides

Andamio is a blockchain-based protocol built on the principle: **flexible rules, standards as truth anchors.**

Every organization defines its own requirements for earning credentials—there's no central authority deciding what's valid. But the infrastructure provides non-negotiable foundations: verifiable credentials are interoperable, tamper-evident, and cryptographically signed. Standards don't tell you *what* to credential or *who* can issue. They tell you *how* to create credentials that are verifiable, portable, and trustworthy.

This is the difference between infrastructure and platform. Platforms have gatekeepers. Infrastructure has standards.

Andamio primitives:
- **Credential issuance and verification** — anyone can attest, effectiveness emerges from use
- **Localized access control** — teams define their own requirements and governance
- **Discoverability and resource sharing** — credentials unlock opportunities across the network
- **Human attestation infrastructure** — signatures that matter in an age of synthetic content
