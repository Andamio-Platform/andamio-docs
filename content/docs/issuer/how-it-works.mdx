An Andamio credential behaves differently from a badge because of a few deliberate design decisions. Each one follows from anchoring credentials on a public ledger instead of inside a single company's database.

## What a credential is

A credential is an ordered list of statements. A statement is anything you are willing to certify: a learning target, a skill, a behavior or a completion criterion. One statement is enough.

The API calls the list `slts`, short for Student Learning Targets, the statements a credential certifies. The name comes from courses, but a credential does not have to be a course. Here is a team-member credential, defined with `POST /courses/{course_id}/credentials/create`:

```json
{
  "alias": "ops-lead",
  "credentials_to_add": [
    {
      "slts": [
        "Completed security awareness training",
        "Shadowed two on-call shifts",
        "Follows the incident reporting procedure"
      ],
      "allowed_student_state_ids": [],
      "prereq_credential_hashes": []
    }
  ]
}
```

Every credential belongs to a course, which groups the credentials one program issues. The response returns a `credential_hash` for each definition. With the course's `course_id`, it identifies the credential.

## Rules for defining a credential

The [Issuer API reference](https://api.andamio.io/issuer/v1/docs/index.html) does not state these rules. Plan for them before you define anything.

- **Each statement is at most 140 characters of plain ASCII.** Text with other characters is rejected.
- **Order matters.** The `credential_hash` is the hash of the list in order. A changed word, a new order, or the same text split differently makes a new credential.
- **Nothing can be edited on mainnet.** To change a credential, define a new one.
- **The list cannot be empty.** One statement is enough. An empty string and a repeated statement are accepted, but they certify nothing useful.
- **One call creates at most 25 credentials.**
- **Send `[]` for `allowed_student_state_ids`**, and for `prereq_credential_hashes` when the credential has no prerequisites. The reference marks both optional, but omitting either fails with `400` "Missing required field".
- **A prerequisite works only within the same course.** A prerequisite that points at another course or another issuer is accepted when the credential is created, and then every learner is refused when they submit evidence.

## The credential model

### It outlives whoever issued it

The credential is immutable and stored on public infrastructure that no single company owns. It cannot be altered after the fact, and it does not disappear when a vendor or contract ends. You are not locked in, and neither are the people you credential.

### It can build on other credentials

A credential can be required before someone takes on the work that earns another credential in the same course. List the required credentials in `prereq_credential_hashes`, so what someone holds opens the door to what comes next.

### Software can act on it

The credential is machine-readable. An application can check that someone holds it and gate access on that, unlock the next step, or drive what happens next. It stops being something to look at and becomes something your systems use.

### Proof is public, evidence is private

Anyone can verify that a credential is real, while the work behind it stays with you. This is how a diploma works: the credential is public, the exam papers are not. With each submission, the learner puts a short reference on chain, at most 140 characters. A hash of the evidence is recommended. The evidence itself stays in your own store.

### You own what it means

The barrier to issue is low, and the credential makes no claim about its own value. You define what it certifies, and its value comes from the track record it earns once it is in the world. Andamio provides the issuance infrastructure. The meaning is yours.

### The earner keeps it

The credential lives with the person who earned it, not in your system, and they can carry it anywhere it is useful, even if they leave your program.

## Who signs

Andamio builds every transaction and sponsors it, so the people you credential never hold ADA. Each person signs their own actions with a social login or a browser wallet: the learner signs their evidence and their claim, and a teacher signs the accept. [Roles and actions](/docs/issuer/actions) lists who signs each action.
